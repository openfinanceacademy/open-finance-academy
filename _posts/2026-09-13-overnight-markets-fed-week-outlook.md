Wall Street is heading into the new week on a cautious footing. U.S. stock futures declined Sunday evening, led by technology shares, while crude oil climbed. The combination puts renewed pressure on investors preparing for Wednesday’s Federal Reserve decision. [Overnight market data](https://www.investing.com/indices/indices-futures), [Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm).

*September 13, 2026 — Outlook for September 14–18. Overnight figures reflect Sunday evening’s available snapshot; U.S. futures quotes were timestamped around 8:45 p.m. Eastern and delayed. Linked market-data pages update over time.*

December Nasdaq 100 futures fell 1.19%, compared with a 0.48% decline for S&P 500 futures. Dow futures slipped 0.12%, while Russell 2000 futures, which track smaller companies, lost 0.15%. European futures also weakened, with Germany’s DAX down 0.29% and the Euro Stoxx 50 off 0.41%. [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/indices/indices-futures).

Energy prices moved sharply higher. West Texas Intermediate crude rose 2.74% to $102.79 a barrel, while Brent gained 3.08% to $107.69. Gold futures fell 0.61% to $4,382 an ounce. Together, these moves suggest an uneasy start to trading, with the largest equity losses concentrated in technology. [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/indices/indices-futures).

The retreat follows Friday’s rebound, when the major U.S. indexes ended a four-session losing streak as oil eased. August consumer-price inflation also came in broadly as expected: headline prices rose 0.4% over the month and 3.4% from a year earlier. That offered some reassurance, although renewed energy pressures complicate the outlook. [Kiplinger’s Friday market report](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/dow-soars-509-points-as-oil-prices-retreat-stock-market-today).

Oil remains a central risk for the coming week. Reporting ahead of the weekend linked elevated crude prices to Middle East tensions and concerns about supply. [Reuters](https://au.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-jumps-oil-lower-ahead-of-fed-vote-next-week-ce785bdfde8af322). If those pressures persist, businesses could face higher transport and production costs, while households have less money available after paying for fuel. That would create an uncomfortable mix of inflation pressure and weaker spending power.

Wednesday is the week’s main scheduled turning point. The Federal Reserve meets September 15–16, with updated economic projections accompanying the meeting. [Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm). The market reaction will depend on both the rate decision and what officials signal about subsequent meetings. A decision that investors expect could still unsettle stocks if the accompanying message points toward higher borrowing costs for longer.

The consumer outlook will receive another test that morning, when the Census Bureau releases August retail sales. [Census Bureau release schedule](https://www.census.gov/retail/release_schedule.html). Strong spending could support company revenue expectations but complicate hopes for easing inflation. Weak spending could raise concerns about growth. The composition will matter: higher spending at gasoline stations, for example, need not mean consumers are buying more goods.

The outlook therefore depends on how these forces develop together. A retreat in oil, steady consumer demand and reassuring Fed guidance could give equities room to recover. Further energy gains combined with a tougher policy message could extend the selling, particularly in shares whose valuations depend heavily on future growth.

Sunday’s weakness establishes a cautious starting point. Whether it develops into a broader decline will become clearer as trading deepens and Wednesday’s economic data and Fed decision arrive.
